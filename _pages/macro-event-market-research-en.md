## The question

Before a central bank announces its decision, we can ask how likely a policy change is and how much of that expectation is reflected in market prices. Once the decision and statement are public, a different question opens up. What surprised the market, and how might the effects travel through rates, currencies, energy, gold and equities over the following weeks or months?

I’m particularly interested in the timing of evidence. A number in today’s economic database may have been unavailable at the forecast cutoff or revised since its first release. Reconstructing what a forecaster could actually see changes which explanations can be tested.

![Research framework linking available evidence and competing explanations to event probabilities and cross-market responses.]({{ '/assets/img/macro-framework-en.png' | relative_url }})

## How I plan to test it

- **Before an event.** Define the question, cutoff and outcome rule in advance. Where a matching market exists, compare the forecast with the crowd probability recorded for the same question at the same time.
- **As an event unfolds.** Compare possible transmission channels, describe the direction and horizon each implies, and record what would count against it. An asset can be evaluated even when it has no corresponding prediction-market contract.
- **Across methods.** Give each method the same evidence and record its forecasts before outcomes are known. The project is developing the method and checking individual cases. Its prospective forecasting performance remains an open question.
